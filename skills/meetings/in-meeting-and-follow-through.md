# Skill: In-meeting responses & post-meeting action follow-through

**Category:** Meetings  
**Theme:** (c) How to respond in meetings; how to close actions after  
**Audience:** Anyone who leaves meetings with “we should…” that never ship  
**Canonical for:** in-room scripts, action-item hygiene (owner+date), 2-hour summary, 對上說不 (room version)  
**Decision language:** reuse [(a) SOR / BLUF](../executive-communication/decision-ready-updates.md)—do not invent a second escalation template  
**Capacity / displace:** [(d) + capacity model](../shared/capacity-model.md)

---

## Mindset shift

Meetings create **alignment and decisions**; they do not create work. Work exists only when there is **owner + deliverable + date**. Your reputation as “someone who makes things happen” comes more from **follow-through hygiene** than from eloquence in the room.

Never surprise your manager in a public meeting. Pre-wire hard news.

**Context split:** This “never surprise” rule is about **your manager** (and rooms where they could be blindsided). Healthy public conflict with **peer teams**—critique plans, not people—is allowed under [(f)](../cross-team/conflict-and-coordination.md) when pre-wired. Do not collapse the two: room scripts protect the boss; (f) keeps partner debate constructive.

## Part A — In the meeting

### Open with decisions, not chronology

**BLUF meeting open:**

> “Three topics. First needs a decision this week: [X]. Second: risk update. Third: FYI. Starting with the decision.”

Put the hardest decision **first** while attention is highest.

### How to respond when put on the spot

| Situation | Weak response | Strong response |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Status asked | Activity dump | Progress / risk / decision needed (3 bullets) |
| Blame / heat | Defend process | Impact + path + what you need |
| New work dumped in | “OK we’ll try” | “We can; it displaces [Y]—confirm priority?” |
| Ambiguous ask | Nod | “What does done look like by when?” |
| You don’t know | Guess | “I don’t have that number; I’ll confirm by [time] and write it back.” |
| Disagreement | Personal | Reframe to shared goal + options |

### Scripts

**Clarify the decision:**

> “Are we deciding scope, date, owner, or just informing?”

**Buy time without looking lost:**

> “I want to give you a clean answer. I’ll validate capacity and come back by EOD with options A/B.”

**Push back on infinite capacity:**

> “Happy to take this. To keep [committed item] on track we need to drop or delay [other]. Which should move?”

**Escalate without dumping** (same shape as [(a)](../executive-communication/decision-ready-updates.md)):

> “Situation: … Impact if no action: … Options: … Recommendation: … Need decision from this group by …”

**Protect the team publicly, coach privately:**

> In room: own the system. Later 1:1: specific feedback.

### 對上說不 / push back in the room

Never bare “no.” Always **yes to the goal + trade-off + decision ask**.

| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| New work mid-quarter | “We can take this. It displaces [committed item] or needs extra capacity. Which priority wins?” |
| Unrealistic date | “To hit that date we need scope cut to [MVP] or two more eng-weeks. Options A/B—recommendation is …” |
| “Just squeeze it in” | “There’s no free capacity in the quarter plan ([link/one-pager]). Silent squeeze = silent slip on [X]. Prefer explicit swap.” |
| Status theater | “Happy to deep-dive offline; for this room: progress / risk / decision needed are …” |
| You disagree with direction | “I can support either path. Trade-off is [risk vs speed]. I recommend B; if we choose A I’ll document accepted risk.” |

**After the meeting:** write the decision (even if “do nothing”) so it doesn’t reappear as fog.

Plan-level pushback and intake rules: [(d) roadmap](../roadmap-planning/planning-sense-proactive-framing.md).

### Contracting your manager (reduces meeting thrash)

Agree in advance:

- What must interrupt them vs batch in weekly digest  
- How they want bad news (Slack first? 1:1?)  
- When you may speak for the team vs when they must be in the room

## Part B — Capture actions *in* the meeting

An action item is only valid if it has:

| Field | Rule |
|-------|------|
| **What** | One concise, observable deliverable |
| **Owner** | **One** person (never “the team”) |
| **When** | Specific date, not “soon / next sprint” vaguely |
| **Done looks like** | One line success criteria |
| **Source** | Link to notes / ticket |

**Live capture phrase:**

> “Before we leave: action is [what], owner [name], due [date]. Correct?”

If no owner volunteers: **assign or escalate**—do not leave orphan actions.

## Part C — After the meeting (the career skill)

### The 2-hour executive summary email / Slack

Within ~2 hours of any significant meeting, send **≤5 sentences**:

1. What was **decided**  
2. What is **still open**  
3. **Who owns** what  
4. **Next checkpoint** date  
5. Links to notes / tickets  

This creates accountability, reduces “I thought you meant…”, and positions you as the person who closes loops.

**Template:**

```
Subject: [Meeting] Decisions + actions — YYYY-MM-DD

Decisions:
- …

Actions:
| Action | Owner | Due | Done when |
|--------|-------|-----|-----------|
| … | @x | date | … |

Open questions:
- …

Next sync: …
Notes: <link>
```

### Personal follow-through system

1. During meeting: star / tag actions.  
2. Immediately after: move **your** items into task tracker with dates.  
3. For others’ items you depend on: calendar reminder **before** their due date (nudge, not blame).  
4. Incomplete items **roll into next agenda** by default.  
5. Designate an action tracker role for recurring forums (can rotate).

### Nudge without drama

> “Friendly ping: [action] was due Friday for [decision Y]. Still on track, or need help / new date?”

## Cadence recommendations

| Touchpoint | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| Pre-read (24h before big decisions) | 1-pager: recommendation + data + ask → meeting decides, not educates |
| In-meeting | Decide + assign |
| ≤2h post | Written decisions/actions |
| Mid-cycle | Nudge owners |
| Next meeting start | Review open actions first (2–5 min) |

---

## Do / Don’t

| Do | Don’t |
|----|--------|
| Lead with decisions needed | Walk the agenda in story order only |
| One owner per action | “Team will handle” |
| Specific due dates | “ASAP” without calendar |
| Write back decisions quickly | Rely on memory / chat scrollback |
| Pre-wire your manager | Blindside them in steering |
| Say “I don’t know yet + when” | Invent numbers under pressure |

---

## Checklists

### Meeting entry

- [ ] Know the one decision you need  
- [ ] Pre-wired stakeholders for contentious items  
- [ ] Data ready for first 90 seconds  

### Meeting exit

- [ ] Every action has owner + date  
- [ ] Decisions restated aloud  
- [ ] Open questions listed  

### Same day

- [ ] Summary sent  
- [ ] Tickets created  
- [ ] Your tasks scheduled  

---

## Success signals

- [ ] Open actions from last meeting are reviewed first (2–5 min) and shrink over time  
- [ ] Stakeholders quote your written decisions instead of “I thought we said…”  
- [ ] New work is swapped in publicly, not via hallway commitments  
- [ ] Your manager is never blindsided by something you already knew
